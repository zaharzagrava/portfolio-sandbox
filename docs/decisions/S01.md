- **S01.D-01** Should registering with an address that already has an account look identical to a new sign-up (no "already registered" error, no instant login)?

  - alternatives: Keep the clear "email already registered" error and the instant login after sign-up, which is friendlier but lets anyone check who is a member. Or return the session for new addresses only.
  - if wrong: Sign-up gets one extra step, which can lower conversion. Every client that expects a session at registration breaks. The opposite choice exposes the member list.

- **S01.D-02** How strict should we be when a refresh token is used a second time (theft protection versus accidental logouts)?
  - default: No grace window. Any reuse of a spent token ends the whole session. If two refreshes arrive at once, one wins and the session is then revoked. The web front-end must send refreshes one at a time.
  - alternatives: Keep the current 10-second grace window, which tolerates double refreshes but lets a thief replay a stolen token in that window. Or use a short window such as 2 to 3 seconds.
  - if wrong: Too strict means customers get logged out unexpectedly, for example by a double-clicking or multi-tab front-end. Too loose weakens theft protection.
  - source: "Reuse-detection grace window (today 10 s default" [BREAKING]
  - status: open

- **S01.D-03** Which ways of proving identity do we stop accepting, which may break existing clients?

  - alternatives: Keep the legacy header for a transition period with a deadline. Or keep old tokens until they expire and then drop them.
  - if wrong: Any app, integration or logged-in user still on the old method is locked out at release. A longer transition leaves the unprotected cookie path open.

- **S01.D-04** What password rules do customers have to meet, and may we send passwords to an outside breach-check service?

  - alternatives: Keep 8 to 72 characters with no breach check. Or require 10 characters and make the breach check blocking, so a failed check means no sign-up.
  - if wrong: Stricter rules lose sign-ups. Weaker rules raise account takeovers. The outside service is a privacy and dependency question.
  - source: "Password policy (today 8–72 chars, bcrypt-era limit)" [BREAKING]

- **S01.D-05** How long do logins last, and how many devices can a user be signed in on?

  - alternatives: Longer or no absolute limit, such as 180 days, for convenience. Or a lower device cap such as 5 to 10.
  - if wrong: Too short means users complain about repeated logins. Too long or unlimited leaves stolen sessions alive. A cap that is too low signs people out of their other devices.
  - source: "Session lifetime: today every refresh extends expiry" [BREAKING]
  - status: open

- **S01.D-06** How many failed logins lock an account, and should the lockout also apply to the real owner?

  - alternatives: A higher limit such as 10 attempts, or a progressive delay instead of a hard block. Or a lockout that needs an email unlock.
  - if wrong: Too tight and a stranger can lock a real customer out on purpose, or shared offices and mobile networks get blocked. Too loose and passwords can be guessed.

  - if wrong: Without reset, locked-out customers go to support. A link that lives too long or passes through a shared channel raises the risk of account takeover.
