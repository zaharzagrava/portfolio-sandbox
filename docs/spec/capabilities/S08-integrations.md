# S08 - Shopify and WooCommerce catalog and stock sync
## Purpose
A shop owner or admin connects a Shopify or WooCommerce store, and its products and stock appear in the marketplace and stay right on both sides. Changes flow in incrementally and exactly once, webhooks make them fast but are never the guarantee, stock is merged from three values so that two sides never ping-pong, bad data is quarantined without stopping the sync, a bad provider is handled by limits, retries and a breaker, and a nightly reconciliation proves that both sides agree. Members see and steer the integration, and it follows the life of its shop.

## Today
The domain `catalog-sync` holds an earlier integration with a test-only `fake` provider, a connect route guarded by `shop.manage`, and job names that start with `integrations.`. This page states the target behavior; the tasks of `specs/domains/S08-integrations/tasks.md` replace the old code, and each rule below is marked `[proof: none]` until its end-to-end file exists.
- The end-to-end files that will prove the rules are `integration-connect`, `integration-sync`, `integration-webhooks`, `integration-stock`, `integration-quarantine`, `integration-resilience`, `integration-reconcile`, `integration-manage`, `integration-lifecycle` and `integration-boundary`, all named `*.e2e-spec.ts` in `packages/backend/libs/domains/catalog-sync`.
- The request, response and event schemas already live in `packages/contracts/src/catalog-sync`.

## Entry points
- The Shopify install route under the shop's integrations, and the anonymous Shopify OAuth callback route (routes are listed in `specs/domains/S08-integrations/contracts/http-api.md` and named here only once the controllers exist).
- WooCommerce connect, list, read, disconnect, pause, resume, sync and reconcile routes of a shop's integrations.
- Quarantine, conflict and reconciliation lists under the integration, with retry and dismiss actions.
- The anonymous webhook route of an integration, raw body.
- Queues `integration-sync` and `integration-backfill`; scheduled jobs `catalog-sync.sync-all`, `catalog-sync.reconcile-all` and `catalog-sync.purge-integration-data`; an administrator may also run `catalog-sync.sync-all`, `catalog-sync.reconcile-all` and `catalog-sync.recover-stalled-imports` through the admin job route.
- Events on topic `catalogsync.events` (the registered name of the earlier `catalog-sync.events`; the event-type patterns allow neither a `-` in an aggregate type nor a `_` in the first segment): `catalogsync.integration_connected`, `catalogsync.integration_status_changed`, `catalogsync.integration_attention_required`, `catalogsync.stock_conflict_opened`, `catalogsync.reconciliation_completed`. The bulk import (S07) uses the same family: `catalogsync.import_finished`.

## Behaviors
- **S08.B-01** Given an admin who starts a Shopify install, when the callback arrives with a valid signature and state, then an integration is created, a backfill starts and the browser lands on the integrations page. [proof: none]
- **S08.B-02** Given a callback with a bad signature, when it arrives, then the answer is the one identical `400` and nothing is stored. [proof: none]
- **S08.B-03** Given a store in another currency, when a seller connects it, then nothing is created and `currency_mismatch` is reported. [proof: none]
- **S08.B-04** Given a product changed at the provider, when a webhook is lost, then the next scheduled pull still applies the change once. [proof: none]
- **S08.B-05** Given stock changed on both sides since the last agreement, when the merge runs, then the result is local plus remote minus base and neither side is overwritten twice. [proof: S08 AS-37]
- **S08.B-06** Given a sale that would take stock below zero, when the inbound change is applied, then a conflict is opened with the numbers and stock stays at zero. [proof: S08 AS-38]
- **S08.B-07** Given a provider item without a price, when it is pulled, then it is quarantined and the rest of the page is applied. [proof: S08 AS-47]
- **S08.B-08** Given a provider that answers 429 with `Retry-After`, when workers meet it, then all pause for that time and none is retried early. [proof: S08 AS-56]
- **S08.B-09** Given a nightly reconciliation that would delete more than the guard allows, when it runs, then it aborts, deletes nothing and raises an alert. [proof: S08 AS-63]
- **S08.B-10** Given a shop that starts offboarding, when its integrations are active, then they are paused by offboarding and resume when it is cancelled. [proof: S08 AS-74]

## Lifecycles
An integration is `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED`, `NEEDS_REAUTH` or `DISCONNECTED`; `DISCONNECTED` is final. A quarantine item is open, resolved or dismissed. A stock conflict is open or dismissed. A reconciliation run is open until it finishes and is then immutable. A sync run ends `completed`, `completed_with_quarantine`, `yielded`, `failed`, `skipped_locked`, `skipped_breaker_open`, `skipped_shop_not_active` or `halted_schema_drift`.

## Owns
- The tables of integrations, their history, installs, cursors, runs, links, quarantine, stock conflicts, webhook receipts and reconciliation runs.
- The queues `integration-sync` and `integration-backfill`, the three scheduled jobs, the five events and the `catalog_sync_*` metrics.
- The rate-limit policies `integrations.shopify`, `integrations.woocommerce`, `catalog-sync.integration-write.shop`, `catalog-sync.sync-now.integration`, `catalog-sync.backfill-now.integration`, `catalog-sync.reconcile-now.integration`, `catalog-sync.webhook.integration` and `catalog-sync.webhook.ip`.

## Decisions
- Webhooks are an optimisation; the scheduled pull and the nightly reconciliation are the guarantee.
- Stock is the only shared field and is decided by one pure merge, so the two sides cannot ping-pong.
- A provider variant is one product, so identity and stock stay one to one.
- No test-only provider exists in production; tests use real HTTP fakes of the provider APIs.
