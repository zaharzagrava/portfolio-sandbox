- **S37.D-01** Should short links be allowed to point only at your own storefront, or at any outside site too?
  - default: only `https` destinations whose host is the storefront host or its `www.` form; anything else is refused with `422 destination_not_allowed`, and a reputation check (2 s timeout) blocks bad destinations.
  - alternatives: allow any `https` site, with the reputation check as the only guard; allow a short owner-managed list of partner domains.
  - if wrong: too strict stops affiliates linking to partner or campaign pages and needs rework of the rules; too loose makes your short domain an open redirect that can be used for phishing.
  - source: "Only `https` destinations (today `http` is accepted)" [BREAKING]
  - status: open

- **S37.D-02** How many active links may one owner hold, and should reaching the limit be a hard refusal?
  - default: 1,000 active, unexpired links per owner; the 1,001st create is refused with `link_limit_reached`; disabling or expiry frees a place.
  - alternatives: a higher or per-tier limit; no cap, relying only on the rate limit.
  - if wrong: too low blocks heavy affiliates and costs support time; too high or none lets one account consume storage and codes without bound.
  - source: "Per-owner cap of active, unexpired links (default 1,000)" [BREAKING]
  - status: open

- **S37.D-03** Should owners be able to edit a link's destination and permanently disable it, and for how long are old codes kept?
  - default: destination-only edit of active links; disable is final (no re-enable); records are kept 13 months after expiry or disable, and the code cannot be reused until then.
  - alternatives: allow re-enabling; keep codes reserved forever; keep a shorter period such as 6 months.
  - if wrong: a final disable cannot be undone by owners and may mean support requests; a short retention could send old posts to someone else's link; longer retention costs storage.
  - source: "New states and routes: `PATCH /links/:code` (destination only)" [BREAKING]
  - status: open

- **S37.D-04** Which visitor data may be kept in each click record?
  - default: country (two letters or empty) and only the origin of the referring page (no path or query), with no address, agent, cookie or user.
  - alternatives: keep the full referrer URL as today (up to 300 characters); drop the referrer and/or country entirely.
  - if wrong: keeping full URLs risks storing tokens or personal data in analytics; too little reduces affiliate reporting value.
  - source: "Click event `version` is `1`, `occurredAt` is the click time" [BREAKING]
  - status: open

- **S37.D-05** Which expired or disabled links should tell the visitor the link is gone, rather than "not found"?
  - default: expired and disabled links both answer `410 link_gone` with identical bodies; unknown codes answer `404`. Expired links used to answer `404`.
  - alternatives: keep `404` for everything; show a branded "link ended" page or redirect to the storefront home.
  - if wrong: visitors from old posts see a bare error instead of a page you control; search engines and clients treat the change differently.
  - source: "Expired links answer `410 link_gone` (today `404`)" [BREAKING]
  - status: open

- **S37.D-06** Should a second client be able to stop clicks being counted (and inflate counts) by sending the "already counted" flag?
  - default: the flag counts only with a shared edge secret; the edge records clicks only for real redirects, not unknown codes. Commission-relevant click counts become trustworthy.
  - alternatives: keep trusting the flag as today; count all clicks at the origin only and drop the edge counting.
  - if wrong: counts can be manipulated (affecting affiliate payouts) or, if misconfigured, all edge clicks are lost; the secret must also be provisioned on the edge.
  - source: "The "already counted at the edge" header is honoured only" [BREAKING]
  - status: open

- **S37.D-07** Should affiliate attribution be limited to a lookup of code → owner, leaving the commission link to checkout to a later capability?

  - if wrong: affiliate commissions may not be creditable at launch, and the cookie window sets how long an affiliate earns after a click; rework spans several capabilities.
