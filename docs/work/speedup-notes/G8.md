# G8 notes (rest of libs/infrastructure)

Nothing here was run. All paths are under packages/backend.

## Changed

### libs/infrastructure/sqs/task-queue.e2e-spec.ts (the example, done before me)
Handlers wait 4 s instead of 15 s, 12 messages instead of 20, and the group runs side by side. Saving about 60 s. Could break: the 2 s claim window is tight on a slow machine.

### libs/infrastructure/projections/consumer-failures.e2e-spec.ts
What changed: tests AS-50 to AS-59 now run side by side (`it.concurrent`, Vitest runs 5 at a time). Nothing else touched, every check and number is the same.
Saving: about 60 s. The 30 s outage wait of AS-59 now overlaps with the nine quick tests, instead of adding up after them (the nine quick ones took about 5 s each).
Why it is safe: each test makes its own topic, its own consumer names (so its own counters) and its own data. AS-59 has its own Redis stand-in on its own port.
Left alone on purpose: AS-60 (tight 1.9 s window), AS-61 (5,000 events, heavy), AS-62 (uses the shared broker proxy and sets it to "refuse"; the file resets that proxy after every test), AS-63, AS-64, AS-65 (6 s session, would suffer if the process is busy). They still run one after another, after the side-by-side group.
Could break: if the machine is slow, ten booting apps at once may make one `waitFor` run into its limit (15 s default). Fix would be to remove `.concurrent` from AS-50 to AS-58 and keep it on AS-59 only.

### libs/infrastructure/projections/replay-rebuild.e2e-spec.ts
What changed: all 8 tests (AS-82 to AS-89) run side by side, 5 at a time. Checks unchanged.
Saving: about 40 to 60 s (the three tests that load 1,000 to 1,500 events, and the other waits for the group to settle, now overlap).
Why it is safe: each test has its own scenario (own topic, own groups, own consumer names, own rows filtered by consumer name). AS-87 swaps the logger for a moment, but it only looks for its own audit line, which no other test writes.
Could break: three tests push 1,000+ events through the same process at once; their wait limits are 60 s, so there is room, but a very slow machine could show a timeout.

### libs/infrastructure/projections/consumer-idempotency.e2e-spec.ts
What changed: AS-28 to AS-37 run side by side, then AS-38 alone (1,000 events, rebalance), then AS-39, AS-40, AS-42 and the second AS-31 side by side. The "reset the fault proxy after each test" hook became "after all tests", because with side by side tests one test finishing would have reset the proxy in the middle of AS-37 (only AS-37 uses the proxy, and it brings it back itself).
Saving: about 50 to 70 s (AS-37 alone is 20 s or more with its 9 s cut; the rest overlap).
Why it is safe: own scenario per test as above. AS-38 stays alone so the heavy 1,000-event run cannot starve the 6 s session of AS-37.
Could break: AS-37 has a 6 s session, if the machine is very busy while AS-35 or AS-32 run, its heartbeat may be late and it could see an extra rebalance.

### libs/infrastructure/projections/kafka-transactions.e2e-spec.ts
What changed: the 3 tests run side by side. Saving about 15 s (each has a fixed 2.5 s quiet-read and several waits).
Why it is safe: own topics, own transactional ids, own groups; the pipeline object keeps no state between runs.

## Looked at and not changed
- serverless/runner/fifo-semantics, dead-letter, local-runner, serverless/workers-wiring: each wait is the queue's redelivery time, and the runner itself refuses a queue whose visibility time is below 6 times the function timeout (so 6 s is the floor). The files also share one list of started runners/queues and one log-capture across tests, so they cannot run side by side without a bigger rewrite.
- lifecycle/graceful-shutdown: the tests measure short times (350 ms, 550 ms, under 2 s) and share one list of child processes that is killed after each test; running side by side would make the timings unreliable.
- projections/read-your-writes: time limits of 300 ms and 500 ms and counters shared by all tests.
- projections/versioned-sinks, observability: no long waits; one big test in observability.
- events/event-publisher: the 10 s hanging-broker wait is the product's own timeout, and the tests share one broker proxy and one publisher.
- idempotency/idempotency, serverless/idempotency: waits are under 1 s each; shared call counters between tests.
- platform/*, llm/*, context/*, net/*, inbox, outbox/*, events/outbox-append, outbox-rows-helper, trace-propagation, bundling, nest-context, define-sqs-handler: no sleeps or loops of waits; they use a fake clock or answer at once. Not worth the risk.
